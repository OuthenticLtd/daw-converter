03 — What travels
Everything you made, the way you made it.

    

      
Fig. 01.cpr · .rpp · .als

      
    
        
  1. 01
    Projects
    Cubase (.cpr), REAPER (.rpp) and Ableton Live (.als), in every direction, with every media file collected.
    2. 
        
  2. 02
    Tracks
    Audio, instrument and MIDI tracks, folders and groups, names, colours, mute, solo, track delay.
    3. 
        
  3. 03
    Clip colours
    Audio and MIDI clip colours in every direction.
    4. 
        
  4. 04
    Mix
    Volume (past +6 dB too), pan with each DAW's pan law matched, sends and returns, output routing into groups (Live's Audio To becomes a Cubase group).
    5. 
        
  5. 05
    Audio clips
    Position, offset, clip gain, volume curves drawn in the clip, fades with their curves, reverse, playback rate, pitch, stretch (pitch kept or tape-style) and warp markers, overlapping clips.
    6. 
        
  6. 06
    Comping
    REAPER lanes and takes, Cubase track versions and Live take lanes carry across — every take kept, the comp playing (Live: take lanes with the comp on the main lane).
    7. 
        
  7. 07
    MIDI
    Notes, velocities, controllers; several instruments layered on one REAPER track (Cubase: a track per instrument under a group; Live: an Instrument Rack on the one track).
    8. 
        
  8. 08
    Automation
    Volume, pan and plug-in parameters.
    9. 
        
  9. 09
    Arrangement
    Tempo with its changes and ramps, time signature, markers, regions (Live: locators); a Cubase project starting before bar 1 keeps that lead-in.
    10. 
        
  10. 10
    Built-in effects
    Each DAW's own effects become the closest built-in effects of the others, settings carried — EQs, compressors, limiters, gates, expanders, de-essers, multiband dynamics, delays, reverbs, chorus, flanger, phaser, tremolo, auto-pan, pitch shift, distortion, saturation, bit reduction, stereo width, gain. Levels are matched by measurement.
    11. 
        
  11. 11
    Third-party plug-ins
    VST3 and VST2 with their settings; CLAP plug-ins as their VST3 build.
    12. 
        
  12. 12
    VST2 ⇄ VST3
    A plug-in can be written as its other build, settings carried (FabFilter, Native Instruments, iZotope, Serum, soothe2, KORG and more — 72 products so far).
    13. 
        
  13. 13
    Melodyne
    Edits come across natively.
    14. 
      

    

  






  

    
04 — Honest limits
What doesn't travel (yet).

    
    
      
  1. Cubase effects with no equivalent elsewhere: Vocoder, VocalChain, Pitch Correct, FX Modulator, AutoFilter, DualFilter, ToneBooster, Grungelizer, Metalizer, RingModulator, StepFilter, Tranceformer — left out with a note; render the track in Cubase first.
    2. 
      
  2. Live devices with no equivalent: Corpus, Resonators, Vocoder, Beat Repeat, Looper, Spectral Resonator, Spectral Time, Erosion, Max for Live devices.
    3. 
      
  3. VST2 in Cubase: Cubase 15 loads only VST3, so a VST2 always arrives as its VST3 build.
    4. 
      
  4. VST2 ⇄ VST3 not yet for: Omnisphere, Stylus RMX, Arturia CS-80 and plug-ins not in the list — they stay in the format they were saved in.
    5. 
      
  5. Plug-ins must be installed on the machine that opens the converted project. Converting to Live on a computer with Live installed picks the build Live can load there (VST2 or VST3); in the browser, choose the build yourself if a plug-in does not open.
    6. 
      
  6. Not sample-identical: pitch-shifted or stretched clips and the "close" effects use each DAW's own algorithms — they sound alike and match in level, but do not cancel exactly. Synths with random elements never repeat exactly, even in their own DAW.
    7. 
      
  7. Live limits: one clip at a time per track (overlaps go on a copy track), fader up to +6 dB (the rest goes on a Utility), no volume curve on a clip (it becomes the track's volume automation over the clip, same sound).
    8. 
      
  8. Fade curves in Live are all S-shaped, so a REAPER or Cubase curve becomes the nearest Live curve (and comes back as it was).
    9. 
      
  9. REAPER project offset (a timeline starting before bar 1) is not carried to Cubase, where the project plays the same without it (measured); a project taken REAPER → Cubase → REAPER comes back without it, so synths that follow the song position can sound slightly different there.
    10. 
      
  10. Colours are matched to the nearest one where a DAW has a fixed palette (Cubase's project colours, Live's 70 clip colours).
    11.
